01 · The university's questions

Not just how to use AI. What does AI mean?

AI invites inquiry across computer science, philosophy, biology, education, the arts, and the professions. These are questions for the university as a whole—not merely for technical specialists.

Intelligence

Must intelligence resemble human thought? What can we infer from observable performance?

Consciousness

Is fluent language evidence of subjective experience? How would we even investigate the question?

Creativity

What distinguishes an original artifact, creative process, and human authorship?

Safety & alignment

Whose objectives should AI pursue? What happens when capability exceeds our ability to supervise?

Why do some artists reject generative AI?

Consider training-data consent, compensation, labor displacement, imitation of style, authorship, and the cultural meaning of making art. These are distinct questions—not one simple attitude toward technology.

02 · Think with AI

Don't just ask for answers.

DELEGATING THINKING ≠ EXTERNALIZING THINKING

Challenge my argument

Examine my argument critically. Do not try to agree with me. Identify hidden assumptions, plausible counterarguments, and evidence that could falsify my claim. Separate facts, inferences, and uncertainties.

Be a Socratic tutor

Act as a Socratic tutor for this topic. Ask one question at a time, wait for my answer, and offer hints rather than immediately supplying the solution. After my attempt, explain the reasoning.

Find what is missing

What might I be overlooking? Identify missing information, alternative explanations, and what evidence would help us distinguish between them. Mark uncertain claims clearly.

Second model as critic

Review this AI-generated work as an independent critical colleague. Check disciplinary accuracy, pedagogical value, accessibility, hidden assumptions, and possible errors. Recommend specific revisions; do not rewrite everything.

06 · Read and discuss

Further reading

Brian Greene

Until the End of Time (2020); The Fabric of the Cosmos (2004). Physics, life, mind, and human meaning.

Gary Marcus

Taming Silicon Valley (2024); with Ernest Davis, Rebooting AI (2019). Reliability, cognition, and AI governance.

Marvin Minsky & Seymour Papert

Perceptrons (1969). A historically influential study of limitations of certain perceptron architectures—not a proof that all neural networks are impossible.

Alan Turing

“Computing Machinery and Intelligence” (1950). A foundational starting point for discussing machine intelligence.

Copy-ready prompt: define scope before writing code

I teach [DISCIPLINE]. My students struggle with [CONCEPT]. I want them to [ACTION]. Propose a small interactive educational artifact that can be built in 30–40 minutes as one self-contained HTML file (HTML, CSS, JavaScript). No login, server, external API or personal data. First clarify the learning objective, one main interaction, feedback, and minimum viable scope. Do not write code yet. Wait for my approval.

Important: A working demo is not automatically a pedagogically sound or scientifically accurate tool. Test content, accessibility, privacy and student experience before deployment.

Five short experiments

1 · Can a model challenge its own answer?

Ask for a persuasive explanation, then request three counterarguments and evidence that could falsify it. What changes?

2 · Do two models agree?

Ask two different models the same disciplinary question; compare assumptions, citations, uncertainty and errors.

3 · What counts as creativity?

Ask a model to generate an unusual solution, then distinguish novelty, usefulness, intention and authorship.

4 · Can a model explain a concept interactively?

Use the scope prompt above to build a one-variable simulation. Predict first, experiment, then explain.

5 · When should an agent stop?

Design an automation that requires explicit human approval before sending messages, changing grades or publishing content.

Before using AI in an academic workflow

Ask: Are data permitted? Is the result verifiable? Who owns the content? What could go wrong? Who approves consequential actions? What happens if the system fails?

Alignment is not all of safety. An agent can pursue an assigned goal while still making factual errors, exposing information or taking unsafe actions. Define permissions, boundaries and review.

Read, watch, and disagree
